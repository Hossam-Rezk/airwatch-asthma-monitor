        Getting started

        1. Prepare the Pico (once). Install MicroPython on the Pico and copy main.py, config.py, sensors.py and alerts.py from the pico folder onto it (for example with Thonny). Close Thonny afterwards.
        2. Plug it in. Connect the Pico to this computer with a USB data cable. Its LED blinks blue while it starts.
        3. Connect. On the Dashboard pick the Pico in the USB port list (it is chosen for you when found) and press Connect.
        4. Watch. The four graphs update every few seconds and the AI panel explains the current asthma-trigger risk. The Pico's LED and buzzer follow the same risk level.

        Risk levels

        SafeConditions are unlikely to trigger asthma.
        WarningPossible triggers. Sensitive people should take care.
        DangerHigh risk. Avoid the area and follow your asthma action plan.

        Troubleshooting

        • No Pico in the list: try another cable or USB port; charge-only cables do not carry data.
        • "Port in use": close Thonny or any other serial monitor, then press Connect again.
        • Connected but no readings: the Pico files are missing or have an error. Open the Pico in Thonny to see the message, then close Thonny.
        • Air quality missing for the first 30 seconds: normal, the gas sensor is warming up.
