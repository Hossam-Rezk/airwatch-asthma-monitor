## Dataset

- **Source:** Kaggle, [Air Quality and Pollution Assessment](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/mujtabamatin/air-quality-and-pollution-assessment) by Mujtaba Mateen, Apache 2.0 licence. Found and downloaded with the Kaggle MCP server.
- **File:** `data/updated_pollution_dataset.csv`, 5000 rows, no missing values.
- **Columns:** temperature, humidity, PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, distance to industry, population density, and the label `Air Quality` (Good 2000, Moderate 1500, Poor 1000, Hazardous 500).
- **Why this one:** small, clean, labelled, and its columns overlap with the four Pico sensors.

## Preprocessing

1. Kept only what the Pico can measure: temperature, humidity, PM2.5, and CO + NO2 for the gas sensor. PM10, SO2, distance to industry and population density were dropped.
2. Clipped humidity to 0-100 % (195 rows were above 100 %) and PM2.5 to >= 0 (0 rows).
3. Built the gas index that matches the MQ-135 `aqi_proxy` (0-500): CO and NO2 converted to their US EPA AQI sub-indices, added, then mapped linearly so the training data's 1st percentile is 0 and its 99th percentile is 300 (anchors 21.49 and 77.02, stored in the model file).
4. Labels merged into the three dashboard levels: Good -> SAFE, Moderate -> WARNING, Poor and Hazardous -> DANGER.
5. Health floor from the US EPA PM2.5 breakpoints for sensitive groups: PM2.5 >= 35.5 ug/m3 is at least WARNING and >= 55.5 ug/m3 is DANGER (144 labels raised). The test set uses the same labels.
6. Stratified 80 / 20 train / test split (4000 / 1000 rows), seed 42.
7. Training split only: 600 extra rows copied from random training rows with just the dust value raised to 36-300 ug/m3 (labelled by the floor above). The raw data never has high dust with otherwise clean air, so without this the model would ignore dust on its own.
8. 3 % of training values blanked at random, so the median imputer learns to cope with a failed sensor.

## Limitations

- The labels describe general air quality, not asthma attacks. The model is a screening aid, not a medical device.
- The dataset is regional outdoor data; the Pico measures one indoor spot with low-cost sensors. The MQ-135 index and the Sharp dust reading are only as good as their calibration in `pico/config.py`.
- Retrain on your own logged readings (`data/readings_log.csv`) once you have reviewed labels.
